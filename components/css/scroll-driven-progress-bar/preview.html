Chapter 01

Reading the water

Every surfer learns it eventually: the ocean tells you what it is about to do, long before it does it. A dark band on the horizon, a ripple that runs against the grain, a channel where the foam refuses to settle. None of it is random. The water is a page, and the people who ride it well are simply the ones who learned to read.

The same is true of a long page on the web. Readers want to know where they are, how far the shore is, and whether the effort of paddling on is going to pay off. That is the whole job of a reading progress bar. It is a horizon line for a document.

Chapter 02

How the bar rides the scroll

Under the hood there is exactly one moving part. The stylesheet creates a named Scroll Progress Timeline on the root element, then hands that timeline to a two-frame animation on the bar. The browser does the bookkeeping: zero percent scrolled means an empty bar, one hundred percent scrolled means a full one, and every position in between maps straight across.

There is no scroll listener throttling to tune, no resize observer to babysit, no layout thrash to worry about. The scroll position is the animation clock. When you stop scrolling, everything simply holds still, costing nothing.

"Not the width is being animated, the element is scaled down on the x-axis using a transform, to leverage composited animations." Bramus Van Damme, scroll-driven-animations.style

Chapter 03

Why transform beats width

An older version of this pattern would animate the bar's width from a script, forcing the browser through style and layout work on every scroll event. This one scales the bar on the x-axis instead. Transforms are composited, which means the fill can be updated on the compositor thread while the main thread is busy parsing, painting or running your application code.

In practice you can throw a heavy page at it, image decoding, hydration, third-party scripts, and the bar keeps gliding at full frame rate. It is the difference between paddling against the current and letting the wave carry you.

Chapter 04

Built to degrade gracefully

The bar starts life collapsed to zero width, and only the browsers that understand scroll timelines are allowed to stretch it. That guard lives in a single at-rule, so a visitor on an older browser never sees a broken or permanently full bar. They just see this article, complete and readable, with a clean top edge.

Reduced-motion preferences get the same respect. If a visitor has asked their system for less movement, the bar stays out of the picture entirely. The content never depends on it, which is exactly how a progress indicator should behave: helpful when present, invisible when absent.

Chapter 05

Make it yours in one line

Thickness, color and stacking are all exposed as custom properties on the bar itself. Want a hairline in deep teal under a fixed header? Set the height to two pixels, the fill to a solid color, and raise the z-index above your navigation. A festival site might want eight pixels of coral gradient instead. Same element, same stylesheet, one inline style attribute of difference.

And because the whole part is a single div and a handful of declarations, it composes cleanly with everything else in the library. Drop it on a case study, a changelog, documentation, any page long enough to earn a horizon line.
