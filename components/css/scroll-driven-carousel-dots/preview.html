Carousel dots that
ride the swipe

Step indicator dots that track a scroll-snapping carousel through a named CSS scroll timeline, no JavaScript anywhere on this page.

Scroll down, then swipe↓

Works in Chrome/Edge 115+ and Safari 26+. Elsewhere the carousels still swipe and snap, with a plain scrollbar as the indicator.

The gliding dot

The teal thumb is not stepping between states, it is scrubbing the track's scroll position live. Drag a slide halfway and watch it hover between dots.

Swipe on touch, two-finger scroll on a trackpad, or focus the carousel and use the arrow keys.

Any slide count, one custom property

This one holds four slides. The rail gets four dots and --sdc-slides: 4, and the thumb's travel distance is computed from the dot size and gap, so it always lands dead center.

The original step bar

The source demo's exact mechanic, kept as a variant. The bar starts at a third of the width (slide 1 of 3) and grows to full width as the track reaches its scroll end.

"The indicator is absolutely positioned inside the scroller, so it stays put while the slides move under it."
The containing block trick

Why scroll-timeline is named

From the original demo notes

Three lines carry the whole effect

Everything else is layout. The timeline wiring is guarded by @supports and prefers-reduced-motion.

1

Publish

The snapping track declares scroll-timeline: --sdc-carousel inline, exposing its horizontal progress by name.

2

Subscribe

The thumb and the bar set animation-timeline: --sdc-carousel, so their keyframes scrub with the scroll.

3

Degrade

Outside the guards the indicators stay hidden and a thin scrollbar appears instead. The carousel keeps working everywhere.
