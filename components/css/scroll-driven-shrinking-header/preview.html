Sand & Surf · Scroll-driven CSS

Shrinking Header with Shadow

Scroll down a little and watch the profile bar above compress, tuck its button, fade its waves and pick up a drop shadow, all within the first 150 pixels of scroll and all in pure CSS.

Best in Chrome or Edge 115+ and Safari 26+. Elsewhere the header simply stays sticky at full size, and reduced-motion users get the calm static version too.

What is actually happening

Five tiny keyframe animations run at once: the identity grid compresses, the name shrinks, the shadow fades in, the round button tucks down and the wave art drifts away.

Every one of them is attached to the same scroll timeline with a short animation range, so the scrollbar is the playhead. Scroll back up and the header grows again in perfect reverse.

1

scroll()

Hooks each animation to the document scroller, no JavaScript listeners anywhere.

2

animation-range

0 to 150px means the whole transformation completes within the first flick of the wheel.

3

@supports

Unsupported browsers keep a plain sticky header with a permanent soft shadow.

Why the header goes fixed

While shrinking, the header must not change the document height, or the very scroll it reacts to would jitter. Inside the @supports block it switches from sticky to fixed, and the next sibling gets a matching margin-top so nothing jumps.

Keep scrolling, it stays put

Once the first 150 pixels are behind you, the header rides along in its compact state. The name, the avatar and the edit button remain reachable the whole way down.

Room to breathe

This section only exists to give the page honest scroll length, the way a real profile page would have activity, posts and settings below the fold.

Calm seas, long lines, a slow tide working its way up the sand. The reef keeps its own rhythm and the light keeps changing. By late afternoon the water turns to hammered glass.

Nobody hurries here, and neither does this header. It did its whole trick in the first 150 pixels and now it simply keeps you company.
