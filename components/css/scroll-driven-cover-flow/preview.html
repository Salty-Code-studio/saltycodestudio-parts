Cover Flow,
pure CSS

The iconic album shelf, rebuilt with zero JavaScript. Each cover rides its own view(inline) timeline: rotated while off-center, face-forward the moment it snaps to the middle.

The shelf below scrolls sideways ↓

Best in Chrome, Edge 115+ or Safari 26+. Elsewhere you get a clean flat shelf, and reduced-motion users do too.

Drag, swipe, use the arrow keys after focusing the shelf, or hold Shift while you scroll. One cover always snaps to center and pops toward you. Your browser shows the flat shelf: the 3D rotation needs scroll-driven animation support (Chrome or Edge 115+, Safari 26+).

Three moves, one shelf

The whole effect is a horizontal snap scroller plus one keyframe track per cover. The browser does everything else.

1

view(inline) timelines

Each cover's animation progress is its own trip across the scrollport, tracked with animation-timeline: view(inline). The 50% keyframe is dead center.

2

One keyframe track

Covers enter at rotateY(-45deg), straighten and pop out with translateZ and scale(1.5) at center, then mirror out the other side.

3

Snap and reflect

scroll-snap-type: x mandatory keeps one cover featured at rest, and -webkit-box-reflect paints the glassy floor, all in CSS.

The trick that makes it stable

The transforms live on the cover art, never on the <li>. If the list items rotated, the scroller's total scroll distance would keep changing and the shelf would flicker.

The <li> boxes stay perfectly still in the scroller; only the art inside them spins. That separation, straight from the original demo, is what keeps the 3D and the scrolling from fighting each other.
