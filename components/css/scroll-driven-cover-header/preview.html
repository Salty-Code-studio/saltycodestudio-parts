Cover card to fixed header

A full-screen cover that shrinks and docks into the page header as you scroll. Pure CSS, zero JavaScript.

↓

One scroll timeline, zero JavaScript

The cover above starts at a full 100vh and collapses to a slim 10vh bar over the first 90vh of scrolling. The animation is scrubbed by the scroll position itself, so easing back up replays every frame in reverse.

Needs CSS scroll-driven animations: Chrome and Edge 115+, Safari 26+, Firefox behind a flag. Elsewhere the cover simply stays a static full-height hero.
1

scroll() timeline

The dock animation is bound to the document scroller with animation-timeline: scroll() and plays across a 90vh animation-range.

2

Fixed, not sticky

The cover is position: fixed, and the content wrapper is padded down by 100vh so the page never jumps when the cover leaves the flow.

3

Guarded by @supports

The pinning and the timelines only activate inside an @supports and reduced-motion check. Unsupported browsers get a clean static hero.

Why not position: sticky?

Sticky seems like the obvious tool, but it fails here for a sneaky reason: the animation shrinks the cover's height, and a sticky element's height is part of the document. Shrinking it would shrink the whole page and eat the scroll distance mid-animation.

So the cover is fixed instead, taken out of the flow entirely, and the content below compensates with a 100vh top padding. That is the whole trick, borrowed straight from Bramus Van Damme's original demo.

The parallax is just background-position

The seascape behind the title is a single inline SVG, delivered as a data URI, that is much taller than it is wide. While the card shrinks, its background-position animates from the top of the artwork to the bottom, so the sun sets and the water deepens as you scroll.

At the same time the keyframes animate background-color toward a deep ink teal. Because the cover uses background-blend-mode: soft-light, that color tints the artwork progressively darker, which keeps the white title readable at every height.

The type shrinks for free: the title is sized in em against the cover's own font-size, and the keyframes animate that one property from hero scale down to header scale.

Field notes from the beach office

Keep scrolling. This section exists to give the timeline room to breathe, and to show how ordinary article content behaves under a docked header: it simply slides beneath the bar like any fixed navigation.

The docking completes at 90vh of scroll, which means everything below this point lives with the finished slim header. Scroll back toward the top at any speed and the cover grows right back to full bleed, frame by frame.

Because the animation is driven by scroll position rather than time, there is nothing to desynchronize. No listeners, no requestAnimationFrame, no jank from work on the main thread. The browser owns the whole effect.

The tagline in the cover fades out over the first 35vh using a second animation on the same scroll timeline. That keeps the docked bar clean: title only, no clutter.

One honest caveat: the keyframes animate height and font-size, which trigger layout. For a single header element that is perfectly fine in practice, but keep heavy content out of the cover itself.
