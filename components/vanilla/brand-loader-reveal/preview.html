Three beats, one entrance

The whole effect is a fixed overlay, one clip-path transition, and a handful of transition-delay classes. No library, no bundle.

1

Hold

The overlay covers the page in one brand color, the logo mark pulses, and body scroll stays locked while assets load.

2

Lift

On window load, after a minimum display time of 1.2s, the curtain wipes upward with a strong cubic-bezier ease and removes itself.

3

Arrive

Hero elements fade in and rise a few pixels, each one a beat later via the .blr-d1 to .blr-d6 delay classes.

Your content is never trapped

Loaders are only charming when they always end. This one resolves in layers, so the page shows up no matter what goes wrong.

  1. 01 window load fires the reveal in the normal case, after the minimum display time.
  2. 02 an 8s safety net reveals anyway if some asset hangs forever.
  3. 03 if the script never runs at all, a pure CSS failsafe lifts the overlay after 4s.
  4. 04 reduced-motion users get a quick fade and the content simply appears, no stagger.

One call, four options

Everything runs through initLoaderReveal(options). Swap the inline SVG for your own mark and you are done.

  • minTimeminimum overlay time in ms, 1200 by default
  • onRevealedcallback fired once the overlay is removed
  • maxWaitsafety net that reveals even if load hangs
  • loaderselector or element, defaults to .blr-loader

Paste it in, four steps

The part is two small files plus a few classes on your hero.

  • Drop the overlayPut the .blr-loader div (with your logo SVG) as the first child of body.
  • Tag the heroAdd .blr-stagger plus a .blr-d1 to .blr-d6 delay class per element.
  • Link the filesstyles.css in the head, script.js before your init call.
  • InitializeCall initLoaderReveal({ minTime: 1200 }) and ship it.
